# Phase 0 Provider Interview Script

Supports: 03-plan.md task 0.1 (10 interviews: 5 small, 5 medium providers)
Feeds: 02-prd.md section 3 (personas) and section 8 (open questions Q1 to Q5)
Status: Draft v0.1
Date: 2026-10-22

## 1. Purpose and rules for the interviewer

Goal: learn how the provider handles compliance today, what went wrong last time, and what they would pay to fix it. We are not pitching the product in these interviews.

Rules:
1. Ask about past behaviour, not hypothetical behaviour. "Tell me about the last incident" beats "Would you report incidents faster with a tool?"
2. Do not describe the product during the first 40 minutes. Describe it only in section 7, and only as a concept card.
3. Do not ask "Would you buy this?" or "Do you think this is a good idea?" Those questions produce polite yeses.
4. Let silences run for 5 seconds before you prompt.
5. Ask "What happened next?" and "How did you know?" after any claim about a process.
6. Never ask for participant names, NDIS numbers, or any identifying detail of a participant. If one is volunteered, stop the person, do not write it down, and note "participant detail given, not recorded".
7. Record each interview with consent, or take notes only if they decline. Delete the recording after transcription (within 30 days, or sooner if the participant asks).

## 2. Before the call

- Send the consent note (section 3) at least 24 hours ahead.
- Check the participant's role and organisation size: small = 1 to 30 staff, medium = 31 to 150 staff. Confirm registration groups if you can (public NDIS provider register).
- Prepare a one-page notes template with the sections in section 8.
- Target length: 45 minutes. Hard stop at 55 minutes.

## 3. Consent note (send in advance, and read aloud at the start)

> Thank you for agreeing to talk with us. We are researching how NDIS providers manage compliance: policies, incidents, worker checks and pricing. This is research, not a sales call. You will not be asked to buy anything today.
>
> The call will take about 45 minutes. We will record it only if you agree, and we will delete the recording after transcription. We will not record participant names or other identifying information. You can skip any question or stop at any time. What you tell us may be quoted in our internal notes, but we will remove your name and your organisation's name from anything we share outside the team.
>
> Are you happy to continue, and are you happy for us to record?

Record the answer to both questions before you start: "Consent to continue: yes or no. Consent to record: yes or no."

## 4. Opening (5 minutes)

1. "Can you tell me about your role, and what a normal week looks like for you in compliance?"
2. "How many staff are there, and which support types do you deliver?"
3. "Which registration groups do you hold?" (Note the answer. Do not ask about participant numbers.)
4. "Who in your team looks after policies, incidents and worker checks?"

Use the answers to tailor follow-ups. Note whether the participant is the owner (Q1 in PRD section 8 asks who buys) or a coordinator.

## 5. Current-state questions (15 minutes)

### 5.1 Policies and procedures
1. "Walk me through the last time you updated a policy. What triggered it, and who approved it?"
2. "Where are your current policies stored, and how do staff know which version is current?"
3. "When did you last review your incident management policy? What did the review change?"
4. "Did you write your policies yourself, buy a template, or get a consultant to write them? What did that cost?"

### 5.2 Worker checks and training
5. "How do you find out a worker's screening check is about to expire? Walk me through the last time that happened."
6. "How do you track mandatory training, and how do you know a worker has finished it?"
7. "What happens to a worker's shifts if their check lapses?" (Listen for how they handle the gap, not just the answer.)

### 5.3 Pricing and claims (keep brief; claims are out of scope for v1)
8. "How do you check that your prices stay within the NDIS price limits each year?"
9. "What happens when the price guide changes on 1 July? Walk me through the last change."

Do not dig into claim lodgement mechanics. Note the answer. It feeds decision 0.5 in the plan.

## 6. Incidents and audits: the stories (15 minutes)

Ask for one real story for each. Let the participant choose the example. Do not ask for participant details.

### 6.1 Incidents
10. "Tell me about the most recent incident you had to report to the NDIS Commission. From the moment someone knew, what happened next?"
11. "How did you work out whether it was reportable, and how long did that take?"
12. "How did you keep track of the deadline? Who was responsible for it?"
13. "Has anything ever come close to a deadline and nearly been missed? What did you do?"
14. "Since the new NDIS Commission Portal opened in October 2026, what has changed in how you lodge reports?" (Note the answer. Verify the portal change separately.)

### 6.2 Audits and assessments
15. "When were you last audited or assessed against the NDIS Practice Standards? Walk me through how you prepared."
16. "What did the auditor or assessor find? What did you fix afterwards, and how long did it take?"
17. "If someone asked you tomorrow for evidence of a compliance control, how long would it take you to find it?"

### 6.3 Pain and cost
18. "Of everything we have talked about, which takes the most time, or causes the most worry?"
19. "How many hours a month does compliance take across your team? Who does most of it?"
20. "What does compliance cost you per year now, in staff time, consultants and software? Rough numbers are fine."

Probe for each cost answer: "What did that cost you, in money or in staff time?"

## 7. Concept card and willingness to pay (10 minutes)

Read this only after section 6.

> We are exploring a tool that helps providers check their readiness against the NDIS Practice Standards, drafts policies that a person then reviews and approves, tracks incidents against the reporting deadlines, and keeps worker checks and training up to date. It would not lodge reports for you or give legal advice.

Then ask:

21. "On a scale of 1 to 10, how useful would that be for your business, and why not higher?" (Ask the "why not higher" part. It usually reveals the real objection.)
22. "What would have to be true for you to try something like this in the next 3 months?"
23. "What would make you stop using it after the first month?"

Willingness to pay (ask only if Q21 scored 6 or above):

24. "Suppose it cost [price band below] per month. Would you expect that to be worth it? Why?"
25. "At what monthly price would it feel expensive but still worth buying? At what price would it feel too cheap to trust?" (Van Westendorp style. Record both numbers.)
26. "Who else would need to agree before you bought something like this?"

Price bands to test (rotate, do not announce them all at once; record which band was shown):
- Band A: AUD 150 to 250 per month
- Band B: AUD 250 to 400 per month
- Band C: AUD 400 to 650 per month

Pricing is a hypothesis (decision D3). Treat these numbers as prompts, not as the price.

## 8. Close (3 minutes)

27. "Is there anything we should have asked and did not?"
28. "Who else in the NDIS sector should we speak to? (Only ask for introductions with the participant's consent. Do not ask for personal contact details of other people in the call.)"
29. "Would you be open to a follow-up in 4 to 6 weeks, or to being a pilot provider if we build something?"

Thank the participant. Confirm how they would like to receive the summary (we will send a short written summary, with no identifying detail).

## 10. Analysis after the 10 interviews

- Count how many interviews name the owner as the buyer (Q1) and how many name a coordinator.
- For each of the 5 key PRD assumptions below, record "supported", "contradicted" or "unclear", with the interview IDs:
  1. Providers lose time or money to compliance work each month.
  2. A provider has had a near-missed incident deadline or an audit gap in the last 12 months.
  3. Providers would pay for a readiness and reminders tool, rather than a consultant.
  4. Providers would accept AI-drafted policies if a person must approve them.
  5. Small and medium providers have different buying triggers.
- Pilot commitments (Phase 0 exit gate): at least 5 providers agree in writing to a paid or priced pilot. Ask for this at the follow-up, not in the interview.
- Exit gate rule (plan section 2): fewer than 3 pilot commitments means stop or pivot before building. Record the reason.

## 11. Known limits of this script

- Interviews rely on recall. Ask for the most recent example, and cross-check with a document where the participant offers one (redacted).
- Providers may describe what they think the interviewer wants to hear. Watch for vague answers like "we're pretty good" and ask for a story.
- The 10 interviews are a qualitative sample, not a market size. Do not extrapolate numbers from them.
- Pricing answers are stated preferences. Confirm with a real pilot offer before setting a price.
- Q14 (portal change) is new. The answers are a sign of confusion or a gap, not a fact about the portal.

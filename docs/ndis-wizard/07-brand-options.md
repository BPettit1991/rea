# Brand Options: Product Name and Relationship to Linkhaus

Supports: scope decisions D7 and D8, plan task 0.6
Status: Draft v0.1, options for decision
Date: 2026-10-22

## 1. Decision (2026-10-22)

Option B chosen: a standalone product with a Linkhaus endorsement. Linkhaus is the owner's business. Ozzie AI was an inspiration, not the parent.

Open: product name. It must not collide with "NDIS Wizard by Ozzie AI."

## 2. Context

- Linkhaus is the owner's business and the parent. Confirm the legal entity name before the brand is built.
- A live product named "NDIS Wizard by Ozzie AI" is marketed at ozzieai.com.au/ndis. Per the owner, it is a different product. Do not reuse the name.
- Rainbow Connections Disability Services is an NDIS provider account in ShiftCare. It is not an official partner. The owner may be interested in a partnership. See section 8.

## 3. Architecture options

| Option | Example | Advantages | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Sub-brand of Linkhaus | "[Name] by Linkhaus" | One parent identity; trust carries across; shared marketing | Liability and IP sit with Linkhaus; a problem with one product affects the parent; harder to sell or spin out later |
| B. Standalone product, Linkhaus endorsement | "[Name]", with "a Linkhaus product" in small type | Clear product identity; separate entity possible; endorsement keeps trust | Two names to maintain; needs clear endorsement wording |
| C. Fully standalone | "[Name]", no Linkhaus link | Cleanest for resale or partnership | Loses the Linkhaus trust; higher marketing cost from zero |

Recommendation for discussion: option B, with a separate company holding the product, subject to counsel's view (06-legal-scoping-brief.md, question 13). It keeps the parent's credibility without putting the product's liability inside Linkhaus.

## 4. Naming criteria

1. Does not use "NDIS" as the distinctive part of the mark (counsel to confirm the rule; question 14).
2. Does not contain "Ready" with a common descriptor that is already registered (Practice Ready is registered in classes 35 and 41, so avoid).
3. Works as a short spoken name for providers.
4. Clear domain and social handles. Check manually: no domain tool is connected in this session.
5. Not a name already used by a live product in the NDIS sector.

## 5. Checks so far (IP Australia quick search, 2026-10-22)

| Candidate | Quick search result | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| NDIS Wizard | No records in the register. Live product by Ozzie AI on the same domain | Drop. Name collision |
| Practice Ready | One registered mark, classes 35 and 41, owned by an individual | Drop. Conflict risk |
| Linkhaus (parent) | No records | Not cleared. Run full search |
| Standards Desk | No records | Not cleared. "Desk" is generic and may be weak. Run full search |
| Readiness Desk | No records | Not cleared. Descriptive. Run full search |

A quick search is not a clearance search. Each shortlisted name needs a full IP Australia search covering similar marks and classes 9, 35, 42 and 44 (software, business services, and health-related services), plus an attorney review.

## 8. Partnership with Rainbow Connections (not an official partner)

The owner may be interested in a partnership. Before any agreement:
1. Keep it arm's length. A partner that is also a pilot customer or data source creates a conflict of interest. Put terms in writing.
2. Do not use its ShiftCare data, staff records or participant information for product design, testing or marketing without a separate data agreement.
3. Record the partnership and any shared commercial terms in the scope and plan. Do not treat it as an interview or as Phase 0 demand evidence unless it follows the script and consent rules in 04-phase0-interview-script.md.
4. Add the partnership to the legal brief (06-legal-scoping-brief.md) as a question on conflicts and data sharing.
